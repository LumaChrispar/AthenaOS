## SIX VOICE PARAMETERS (SRV-028 Protocol)

| Parameter | Value | Operational Definition |
|-----------|-------|------------------------|
| **1. Narrative Voice / POV** | Third Limited Omniscient (Shifting POV) | Camera rests behind one character's eyes at a time — Elara's analytic scrutiny, Liam's sensory immersion, Martha's calcified control. Shifts occur at chapter breaks or hard scene breaks marked by ***; never mid-scene head-hopping. Psychic distance flexes: tight during memory intrusions (sentence-level interiority), pulled back during investigative sequences (behavioral observation). |
| **2. Tense** | Past Tense | Strict past tense. No historical present. Flashbacks rendered in past perfect for the first two sentences, then simple past for immersion. No future tense intrusion. |
| **3. Tone / Emotional Register** | Atmospheric, Dread-filled, Melancholic | The default register is a low hum of dread — not jump-scare horror but the dread of recognition: *you have felt this before, you have lost this before, and you will lose it again.* Melancholy is the dominant affect; terror is its periodic spike. Irony is suppressed; humor, when it appears, is gallows-dry and character-bound (Elara's brittle wit, Martha's brittle control). |
| **4. Atmosphere / Sensory Texture** | Decay, Dust, Overwhelming History, Oppressive Beauty | The house breathes dust. Every room holds a scent: mildew and beeswax, dried lavender and copper, the particular mineral smell of cold stone sweating in summer. Light behaves wrongly — pooling, hesitating, swallowing color. Sound travels wrong: footsteps above when no one walks above; a child's laugh in a nursery empty for fifty years. The air is thick, viscous, pressing against skin like wet velvet. |
| **5. Sentence Architecture / Rhythm** | Gothic Cadence — Breathing Sentences | Default sentence: long, hypotactic, clause-stacked, moving like a slow exhale through a dust-choked corridor. Subordinate clauses accumulate sensory detail (dust, light, sound, smell) before the main clause lands the emotional blow. *Then* — a short, sharp, monosyllabic sentence. A knife cut. The rhythm mimics the house's breathing: long inhale (description, memory, dread), sudden exhale (violence, revelation, silence). Fragments permitted only in memory-intrusion sequences or panic states. |
| **6. Psychic Distance / Interiority Gradient** | Variable — Close Third in Memory, Pulled Back in Investigation | **Close (Psychic Distance 1-2):** Memory intrusions, sensory hallucinations, grief flashes — free indirect discourse, sensory fragments, sentence fragments. **Medium (3-4):** Standard scene navigation — behavioral observation filtered through POV character's psychology (Elara catalogues; Liam senses; Martha assesses). **Distant (5):** Transitional passages, house geography, time jumps — omniscient camera, atmospheric establishing shots. No omniscient commentary on theme; theme emerges only through pattern. |

## 500-WORD CALIBRATION PROSE SAMPLE

The front door did not swing. It *exhaled*.

Elara had braced for resistance — warped wood, swollen frame, the stubborn groan of a house refusing entry. Instead, the massive oak panel sighed inward on hinges that had not known oil in half a century, releasing a breath of air so thick with dust and dried lavender and something older, something mineral and cold, that she tasted iron at the back of her throat.

*Calcium. Limestone. Bone.*

She stepped across the threshold and the house swallowed her footsteps. No echo. No creak of protest. The entrance hall drank sound the way dry earth drinks rain — greedily, silently, leaving no evidence of the transaction. Ahead, the staircase curled upward into shadow like a question mark carved from dark oak. To her left, a doorway gaped: the drawing room, its shutters thrown wide, yet the light that spilled across the threshold was wrong. Thin. Watery. It carried no warmth.

*North-facing. Of course. The cold side of the house.*

Elara's fingers found the notebook in her pocket. *Grounding object. Tactile anchor.* Her therapist's voice, not her own. She withdrew it, flipped to a fresh page, clicked her pen. *Observe. Record. Categorize. Control.*

"Charming," Liam said behind her.

She did not turn. His voice arrived thin, reedy — stripped of its usual warmth by the same hungry air. When she glanced sideways, he stood framed in the doorway, sketchbook clutched against his chest like a shield. His eyes, usually the color of storm-water, had dilated until the irises were swallowed. He was not sketching. He was *feeling* the room.

"It's not charming," Martha said from the threshold of the drawing room. She did not enter. She stood as she always stood: spine rigid, hands folded at her waist, the perfect portrait of a woman who has never been surprised by anything. "It is *enduring*. There is a difference."

Dust motes danced in that sickly light. Elara watched them spiral, suspended in currents no window could explain. *Convection currents. Temperature differential. Physics.*

*Physics does not explain the smell.*

She uncapped her pen. The nib touched paper.

*Entry 1. 0900 hours. Front hall. Air quality: particulate density high. Olfactory anomaly — mineral, ferrous, organic decay beneath floral masking agent (lavender, dried). Auditory anomaly: sound absorption coefficient anomalous. Thermal anomaly: ambient temperature 12°C below exterior. Occupant reactions: Subject L (Liam) — hypersensitivity, pupil dilation, psychomotor retardation. Subject M (Martha) — performative control, micro-tension in masseter muscle, avoidance of interior spaces.*

Her hand moved fast. Faster than thought. *Control through taxonomy.*

Behind her, Liam exhaled. A sound like a leaf falling on stone.

"Something died here," he said. Not a question. "Something is *still* dying."

Martha's smile did not waver. "Everything dies, Liam. This house simply remembers it better than most."

The dust motes continued their slow, impossible dance.

Elara wrote: *Subject M displays knowledge inconsistent with stated narrative. Flag for investigation.*

The house breathed.

Dust settled on her notebook.

*Entry 1 complete.*
